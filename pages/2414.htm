Updated 16th April; 10th May; 4th, 21st October 2008; 14th September 2021; 19th May 2022; 23rd September 2023; 27th January, 21st May, 16th November 2025; 20th January, 17th June 2026

   The site lies in a prominent depression set against a cliff, well hidden in the jungle but, since the summer of 2021, easily reached from a logging track. There are three entrances, sharing the depression, which between them give access to the original near-surface cave and to the deep boulder-choke system reached through the wet pitches dig.  A 5m slither (or handline) down through brambles and over domestic rubbish reaches the base of the depression and the three holes.
   The original cave (# 5461) An obvious opening to the SE leads immediately into a crawl, which enlarges to a hand-and-knees crawl and a 3m climb down over collapse to the deepest point. The cave is developed on a fault, and the short throw and orbitolina bed are clearly seen at the top of the short climb.
   The fridge-door hole (# 5462) On the right (north-western) side of the depression, another entrance on the face was enlarged to gain entry to a boulder and mud area. A fridge door now marks this entrance.

   The wet pitches dig (Cueva Regolfo, #2414) and the system beyond.
   Three metres to the left of the fridge door, a stream was heard falling through the 'solid' rock face. This was excavated to form the entrance crawl, Trayectoria de Vuelo (Flight Path), which leads to the awkward head of the first pitch — a head-first, flat-out squeeze out over the drop before clipping in and swinging onto the rope. This first pitch, Puerta Uno (Gate one), drops into the first chamber, Sala de Sónica (Sonic Boom Chamber), where two waterfalls enter. From the chamber, a capped-out crawl, La Pista (The Runway), continues the strongly draughting way on to the head of the second pitch.
   The 5m second pitch, Puerta Dos (Gate two), lands in the second chamber, Salón de Embarque (Departure Lounge), a large boulder-choke chamber. The only solid wall here is the one followed in from above - the continuation of the cliff face. The remainder of the chamber is a mass of transit-van-sized boulders held up on crumbling piles of choss, and most of the ways on lead down into this loose ground.
    Off Salón de Embarque: Vuelo con turbulencias (Bumpy Flight). A traverse over the chamber reaches a series of three short pitches down (7, 5, 3m) what feels like the top of a rift. This looked promising but choked, with the draught disappearing into the boulders in the floor. A narrower, looser continuation heads north-east into the choke, where several holes were pushed before they closed down.

   The main way down. Near the base of Puerta Dos, a route was forced beneath the pitch past a loose cobble bank into a roomier, steeply descending area, where a visual connection was made down into the floor of Salón de Embarque. Rather than rely on the steep, wet and loose squeezes here, a safer line was opened alongside them: the third pitch, Puerta Tres (Gate three), a snug 3m drop that bypasses the cobble slope and squeezes to reach the lower level.
   The lower chambers, Algo que Declarar (Something to Declare), descend through the boulder choke to the lowest point reached, where the digs Canal Rojo (Red Channel) lie. Here a very loose climb splits into two further climbs, each tighter and looser than the last. The cave bottoms out roughly 50m below the entrance, estimated to lie about 40m above the level of Cueva del Torno (#2366).
   In all, the three pitches giving access to the lowest point are approximately 7m, 5m and 3m. A strong draught runs through the cave throughout — pleasantly warm and inward in hot summer weather - and is one reason the dig was pursued for so long.

Exploration history
   The original cave (#5461), developed on a fault with the orbitolina bed and short throw visible at the top of its short climb, was the first part of the depression to be entered. On the north-western side of the depression, a second entrance on the face was later enlarged into a boulder and mud area, and a fridge door placed in its entrance (#5462). The three openings were eventually allocated separate codes - 5461 (the original cave), 5462 (the hole behind the fridge door) and 2414 (the wet pitches dig).
   At Easter 2008, because of the large quantities of water around, a stream was heard falling through the cliff face about 3m to the left of the fridge. This was excavated through a crawl to the awkward head of a pitch. The drop descended 7m into a chamber with two waterfalls entering — now Sala de Sónica, reached by Puerta Uno. It was too wet to excavate at the base of the chamber.
   In the summer of 2008, two possible digs were investigated but stronger measures were clearly required. The water disappeared down a cobble-filled crawl, and there was also a less promising but drier tube further back in the chamber; the wetter dig, on a solid-looking floor, had turned to thixotropic mud, while the drier dig connected with the cobble-filled wet way on. Both draughted strongly. Lying flat out in the tight, water-filled tube allowed water to be pushed forward and out of sight around a bend in a bow wave; the water could be heard falling for a short distance, then a silence of about five seconds, then a further, much deeper sound of falling water - a strong hint of the depth to come.
   Over three trips in August 2021, the entrance crawl (Trayectoria de Vuelo) and the pitch head were enlarged, and the strongly draughting wet way at the base - La Pista - was enlarged and lengthened to a point where a couple more days of capping would allow a look around the next corner. The 2008 "falling water" trick could not be replicated.
    At Easter 2022 the entrance crawl was dug out, with the aim of removing a pool of water part-way along.
    Further work followed in July and August 2023, when the dig was found to be draughting strongly inwards.
   A surface survey was carried out at Easter 2025 to better fix the three entrances. In early November 2025, more digging was done at the base in a very powerful draught, and the surrounding caves and shafts were surveyed or resurveyed (2976, 2977, 5461 and 5462), the sites being brought together in a single, five-entrance Survex file (2414.svx). The site was dug on four occasions around Christmas 2025, with a draught out on at least two of those trips.
   The dig finally broke through in May 2026. The capped way on was pushed to a step and then to the second pitch, Puerta Dos (5m), which dropped into a large boulder-choke chamber, Salón de Embarque - a chamber with only a single solid wall, the rest being levitating, transit-van-sized boulders and smaller rocks. Over the following days the choke was explored: a traverse and a series of short pitches down the top of a rift (Vuelo con turbulencias) choked with the draught lost into the floor, and a narrower, looser continuation heading north-east into the choke was pushed at several holes before sense prevailed amid a feeling of certain entombment.
   Attention then returned to the floor of Salón de Embarque, where a loose cobble bank near the base of Puerta Dos was passed into a roomier, steeply descending area, giving a visual connection back down into the chamber. A safer line was forced down this - the snug 3m third pitch, Puerta Tres - bypassing several steep, wet and loose squeezes to reach the lower chambers, Algo que Declarar. The final lead at the lowest point, the Canal Rojo digs, was pushed down a very loose climb that split into two further, tighter and looser climbs. Any further progress here would mean digging in confined and highly unstable ground rather than simply removing infill.
    These May 2026 trips (video) added 169m of new passage, bringing Cueva Regolfo to a surveyed length of 195m and a depth of 50m, with the lowest point estimated at about 40m above the level of Torno.
The unstable nature of the cave
   The stability of Cueva Regolfo is not to be underestimated. Beyond the entrance series the "cave" is, in truth, the chaos of an enormous boulder choke: from Salón de Embarque downwards the new extensions have only ever had a single solid wall - the continuation of the cliff face followed in from above - while the rest is a mass of levitating, transit-van-sized boulders held up on crumbling piles of choss.
   Almost every metre of the 169m of new passage had to be gardened, stabilised, rearranged or dug out before it could be passed, and many seemingly settled blocks, some a cubic metre or more in size, were seen to move substantially during exploration. The whole of the lower system - Vuelo con turbulencias, Puerta tres, Algo que Declarar and the Canal Rojo digs - lies within this loose ground.
   Exploration took place during a very dry spell. As the cave can take a great deal of water, the sections so far explored are expected to shift after the next flood, and conditions underground may differ significantly from those described above. Extreme caution is advised throughout, particularly in the boulder choke below Salón de Embarque.
